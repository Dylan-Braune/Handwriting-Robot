Each surviving ink blob is treated as a connected
component with a bounding box and centroid.
Components in a plausible single-line size range
are chained left to right. The next candidate to its
right is chosen by minimising a cost that
combines the horizontal gap between them and
vertical deviation. Chains that end up close
together in their local vertical position across their
shared horizontal span get merged, since a single
line is often split into multiple chains by a stray
gap. Dots, ascenders, descenders or multi-line
components are assigned afterward by finding the
nearest line's locally-fitted baseline curve.
Each component is scored for how "diagram-like"
it is by two independent tests: filling any
enclosed holes in its shape and measuring the area
of the largest single hole relative to the square of
the estimated text height. A second test is used
to separate components that are very wide, moderately
tall, and sparsely filled with ink. Components that
score high on either test get flagged as non
text blocks
