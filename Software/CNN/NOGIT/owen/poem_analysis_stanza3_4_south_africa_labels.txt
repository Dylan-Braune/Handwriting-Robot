0	Stanza 3
1	The poem now moves away from natural elements and focuses
2	on people and their daily struggles.
3	Domestic workers making use of public transport to make their
4	way to their menial jobs.
5	Children crying potentially due to hunger or home alone.
6	Note the poignant statement of "the world is so hungry"
7	emphasising the difficulties faced by South Africans.
8	Despite this, at least "madam's house is clean."
9	These lines reveal the vast discrepancies between the lives
10	of the oppressed and wealthy.
11	The women return, tired, to their hungry children after
12	servicing the wealthy.
13	Tone: Ominous and foreboding
14	Stanza 4
15	Old men stand on corners in the rain - the storm has started
16	and these men are subject to it.
17	Tombstones engraved with open eyes: Metaphor:
18	Links these men to the concept of death but also their
19	absolute loss of hope. It could be a little more literal as
20	well, referring to how still and stoic the men are
21	standing.
22	The men have endured the storm for long.
23	The bright cars stand in contrast to the public
24	buses. Note the reference to the bright colours.
25	The men are satisfied to the maximum, which contrast
26	with the "hungry" world.
