0	5. Line Grouping
1	Each surviving ink blob is treated as a connected
2	component with a bounding box and centroid.
3	Components in a plausible single-line size range
4	are chained left to right. The next candidate to its
5	right is chosen by minimising a cost that
6	combines the horizontal gap between them and
7	vertical deviation. Chains that end up close
8	together in their local vertical position across
9	shared horizontal span get merged, since a single
10	line is often split into multiple chains by a stray
11	gap. Dots, ascenders, descenders of multi-line
12	components are assigned afterward by finding the
13	nearest lines locally-fitted baseline curve.
14	Diagram vs. text separation
15	Each component is scored for how "diagram-like"
16	it is by two independent tests: filling any
17	enclosed holes in its shape and measuring the area
18	of the largest single hole relative to the square of
19	the estimated text height. A second test is used
20	to separate components that are very wide, moderately
21	tall, and sparsely filled with ink. Components that
22	score high on either test get flagged as non
23	text blocks.
