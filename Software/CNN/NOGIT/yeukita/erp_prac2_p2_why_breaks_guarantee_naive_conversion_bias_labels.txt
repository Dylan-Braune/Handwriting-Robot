0	3. Why Prac 2 breaks the guarantee:
1	- sensor doesn't measure x and y directly. It measures range r
2	and bearing beta:
3	x = r cos(beta),   y = r sin(beta)
4	This is nonlinear. The Kalman filter requires a linear measurement
5	equation (z = Hx + v). . . have 2 options: run a nonlinear filter
6	(EKF/UKF), or convert the polar measurement into Cartesian
7	coordinates first, and then use a normal linear KF on the converted
8	measurement - my research is on the second approach CMKF family
9	4. Naive conversion and why it is biased.
10	- take noisy rm, betam and compute:
11	xm = rm cos(betam),  ym = rm sin(betam)
12	this seems fine but isn't - cosine and sine are nonlinear functions of
13	a random variable, and the expectation of a nonlinear function of a
14	random variable is not the function of the expectation
15	E[cos(betam)] != cos(E[betam])
16	betam = betatrue + noise. When you average cos(betam) over many noise
17	realisations, the noise does not cancel out symmetrically because cosine
18	curves - averaging pulls result slightly towards zero. This means xm, ym
19	computed this way are BIASED - systematically off, not just noisy.
20	Feed biased measurement into a KF and you get a biased state estimate,
21	no matter how good the filter math is.
22	5. UCM - fixing that bias
23	- fixed w/ a compensation factor
24	- since bias shrinks by a predictable amount (related to sigma_B),
25	you can multiply by the inverse of that shrinkage.
26	xm^u = e^(sigma_B^2/2) rm cos(betam)
