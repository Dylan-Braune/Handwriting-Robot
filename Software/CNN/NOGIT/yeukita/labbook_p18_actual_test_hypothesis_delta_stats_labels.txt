0	-> The actual test:
1	1. Set up the hypotheses: testing whether algorithm1 is better than
2	algorithm 2, where "better" means lower cost.
3	H0: Delta <= 0 - algorithm 1 is NOT better than algorithm 2 (no real
4	improvement)
5	H1: Delta > 0 - algorithm 1 is better than algorithm 2
6	2. Compute the sample mean & standard error of the differences:
7	Delta_bar = (1/N) sum_{i=1}^{N} Delta_i
8	sigma_Delta = sqrt[ (1/N^2) sum_{i=1}^{N} (Delta_i - Delta_bar)^2 ]
9	Delta_bar is just "on average, how much better was algorithm one across
10	all runs" and sigma_Delta is "how much noise/uncertainty is in that
11	average" - it shrinks as you add more MC runs
12	3. Form the test statistic:
13	This is "how many standard errors away from zero is the average difference"
14	if the improvement is real and consistent, this no. will be large. If it's
15	just noise, it will hover near zero.
16	4. Compare against threshold mu_0 from the normal distr. upper tail:
17	mu_0 = 1.64 for 5% sig. level
18	mu_0 = 2.33 for 1% sig. level.
19	If mu > mu_0, you accept H1 - alg 1 is statistically significantly better.
20	If not, you don't have enough evidence, because it properly accounts
21	for correlation b/t the 2 filters' runs.
