- The poem now moves away from natural elements and focuses
on people and their daily struggles
- Domestic workers making use of public transport to make their
way to their menial jobs.
- Children crying potentially due to hunger or home alone.
- Note the poignant statement of "the world is so hungry"
emphasising the difficulties faced by South Africans.
- Despite this, at least "madam's house is clean."
- These lines reveal the vast discrepancies between the lives
of the oppressed and wealthy.
The women return, tired, to their hungry children after
servicing the wealthy.
- Tone: Ominous and foreboding
- Old men stand on corners in the rain - the storm has started
and these men are subject to it.
- Tombstones engraved with open eyes: Metaphor:
- Links these men to the concept of death but also their
absolute loss of hope. It could be a little more literal as
well, referring to how still and stoic the men are
standing.
- The men have endured the storm for long.
- The bright cars stand in contrast to the public
buses. Note the reference to the bright colours.
- The men are satisfied to the maximum, which contrast
with the "hungry" world.
